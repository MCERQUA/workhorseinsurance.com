Insuring the truck is only half the equation. For any business that transports goods, materials, or equipment, the load itself is a valuable asset moving down the highway every day. A vehicle policy protects the truck. It does not protect what is inside it. That is the job of motor truck cargo insurance, and for many operations it is the difference between a manageable claim and a catastrophic loss.

## What Motor Truck Cargo Insurance Covers

Motor truck cargo coverage protects the property you are hauling against loss or damage while it is in transit and in your care, custody, and control. If the goods are damaged or destroyed during a covered event, the policy responds to the value of that cargo rather than the truck carrying it.

The classic covered causes of loss include:

- **Collision and overturn.** If your truck is in an accident and the load is damaged as a result, cargo coverage steps in.
- **Fire.** Damage to the goods from a fire affecting the vehicle or load.
- **Theft.** Stolen cargo, whether the entire load or a portion of it, subject to the policy's specific theft terms.
- **Striking of a load.** Damage caused when the cargo shifts or strikes something during transport.

For a business hauling building materials, finished products, appliances, or specialized equipment, this coverage protects the very thing your customers are paying you to move or supply. Without it, a single serious incident can leave you owing for goods that are now worthless, with no insurance behind the loss.

## Why the Truck Policy Is Not Enough

It is a common and understandable assumption that a commercial auto or physical damage policy covers everything connected to the truck. It does not. Physical damage coverage pays to repair or replace the vehicle. It does not pay for the contents being transported. If a loaded truck rolls over and the load is destroyed, the physical damage portion of your policy addresses the truck while the cargo, often worth far more than the deductible, goes uncovered unless you carry motor truck cargo insurance.

This separation exists because hauling goods is a distinct exposure. The value, fragility, and theft-appeal of a load vary enormously from one shipment to the next, so carriers price and underwrite cargo as its own coverage.

## Exclusions and Limits to Watch Closely

Motor truck cargo policies are not blanket guarantees, and the fine print matters. Owners who do not read the conditions can be surprised at claim time. Pay attention to several common areas.

### Excluded Property

Many policies exclude certain categories of cargo unless specifically scheduled. These can include high-value goods, money and securities, live animals, certain hazardous materials, and contraband. If your business ever hauls something out of the ordinary, confirm it is covered rather than assuming.

### Refrigeration Breakdown

If you haul temperature-sensitive goods, a standard policy may not cover spoilage from a refrigeration unit failure unless reefer breakdown coverage is added. This is a frequent and expensive gap for businesses moving perishables.

### Care, Custody, and Control

Cargo coverage generally applies while the goods are in your control during transit. Loss that occurs while the cargo sits in a warehouse, at a terminal, or otherwise outside your custody may fall outside the policy, depending on its terms.

### Theft Conditions

Theft is covered, but often with conditions. Some policies require the vehicle to be locked, attended, or parked in a secured area for a theft claim to be honored. Understand these requirements before you need them.

### Choosing the Right Limit

The limit you choose should reflect the maximum value of cargo your trucks carry at any one time, not an average. If your trucks routinely carry loads worth more than your policy limit, you are underinsured on your biggest days. Review your typical and peak load values honestly and set the limit accordingly. Deductibles also apply per loss, so factor those into how you set the limit.

## How Cargo Coverage Fits the Whole Program

Motor truck cargo works alongside the rest of a work-truck program rather than in place of any part of it. Commercial auto liability handles injury and damage to others, physical damage handles your trucks, general liability handles your broader operations, and tools and equipment coverage handles the gear that travels to the job. Motor truck cargo fills the specific gap for the goods being hauled. Together these coverages keep a single incident from cascading into multiple uncovered losses.

A few practices help keep cargo coverage working for you:

- **Match the limit to peak load value.** Revisit it whenever the value of what you haul changes.
- **Document your loads.** Good records of what you carry speed up claims and support accurate valuation.
- **Mind the theft conditions.** Train drivers on locking, parking, and securing the vehicle so a theft claim is not denied on a technicality.
- **Disclose unusual cargo.** If you take on a new type of freight, tell your agent so coverage can be confirmed or adjusted.
